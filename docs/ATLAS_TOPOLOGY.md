## Numerical definitions and limitations

The SSH Bloch Hamiltonian uses `q(k)=t₁+t₂ exp(i k)` and energies ±|q|.
Winding is 1 for |t₂|>|t₁|, 0 otherwise, and undefined when the bulk gap
`2||t₁|-|t₂||` closes. A separate 2N×2N open-chain eigensolve gives the
mid-spectrum pair and its site density. This is a finite-size edge diagnostic,
not proof of an exactly zero-energy state at finite N.

The QWZ Hamiltonian is `sin(kx)σx+sin(ky)σy+(m+cos(kx)+cos(ky))σz`.
The occupied lower-band Chern number is computed with normalized eigenvector
links and plaquette phases (Fukui–Hatsugai–Suzuki), on a periodic momentum
grid. The mass-sign phase diagram independently checks whether that mesh
resolves the phase; a disagreeing raw lattice integer is labelled unresolved.
An independent analytic lower-band Berry-curvature formula is integrated
by midpoint quadrature; its deviation from the mass-sign value is shown as a
grid convergence diagnostic. The bulk gap is `2 min(|m+2|,|m|,|m−2|)`. At
`m=−2,0,2`, both invariant and curvature map are reported undefined.

This is a 2D lattice model, not a 3D Weyl solver. Chapter 62 illustrative
figure generators are not used as numerical validation. Tests cover SSH
dimerization, edge splitting and gap closure; QWZ Chern phases, critical
masses, mesh convergence and the then-64 KiB worker response limit. Both labs use
the existing versioned job/result contracts, Electron supervisor, saved runs,
CSV/SVG/manifest exports and workspace snapshots.

## Atlas BHZ bulk and finite ribbon

The additive `bhz_general` job evaluates the Atlas four-band continuum form
`diag[h(k),h*(-k)]`, with `h(k)=(C-Dk²)I+A kx σx-A ky σy+(M-Bk²)σz`.
It also computes a separately declared square-lattice regularization with
`sin(k)` replacing linear momentum, `2(2-cos kx-cos ky)` replacing `k²`, and
lattice spacing `a=1`. `R=0` is the exact block-diagonal Atlas form. The
optional `R τx(kx sx+ky sy)` continuum term (and its sine-regularized lattice
counterpart) mixes the time-reversed blocks while preserving inversion and
time-reversal symmetries. Nonzero R is an explicit extension of the pinned
block-diagonal Atlas formula.

The bounded job stores four continuum bands, four lattice bands, normalized
complex bulk eigenvectors at one declared momentum, a finite open-y ribbon's
full energy/edge-weight samples, and four ribbon eigenvectors at a declared
momentum. The ribbon is a finite sample, not a semi-infinite material edge.
Complex eigenvector phases are gauge dependent; degenerate eigenbases must not
be interpreted as uniquely resolved spin textures. The Fu–Kane inversion
parity Z₂ is emitted only when the producer's gap and mesh checks resolve it.
At a gap closure or unresolved mesh the value is null. Block Chern numbers are
reported only when `R=0`.
The reported bulk gap is a minimum direct band separation. The inspector also
derives the indirect lattice energy gap from saved bands; a nonpositive value
does not establish an insulating Fermi level even when the valence-band Z₂
remains defined.

Runs reopen by hash and export separate continuum and lattice/ribbon `.qscene`
bundles with shared source-run SHA-256. The desktop and web accept A/B/C/D/M,
bounded mixing, momentum grid, ribbon cells and samples. The desktop links a
selected saved ribbon state to its stored density, spin and complex amplitudes.
The original fixed-coefficient `bhz` job remains available for old runs.
The supervisor bounds JSON-RPC responses at 256 KiB; incoming jobs retain
their 64 KiB limit. Producer, schema, saved-run, scene and tamper tests cover
the larger result.

## Bounded BBH bulk and finite corners

The `bbh` native job follows the pinned Atlas four-orbital, π-flux formula.
Its explicit Hermitian representation uses Γ₁,₂,₃ = −τᵧ ⊗ σₓ,ᵧ,𝓏 and
Γ₄ = τₓ ⊗ I. Positive λₓ, λᵧ are bounded to 0.25–2, γₓ, γᵧ to ±2,
the periodic momentum grid to 11–21 points per axis and the finite open
square to 3–7 unit cells per side. Energies use the normalized Atlas scale.

The worker stores four sampled periodic bands, their minimum sampled direct
gap, four finite-system midgap energies, and the mean cell density of those
four normalized eigenstates. Corner weight sums the four exposed corner
cells. Degenerate eigenvectors can rotate within their subspace, so the
four-state mean is the stable visualization. Corner localization is a
finite-size observation. New runs separately compute occupied-band Wilson
loops in both directions and nested polarizations of their gapped Wannier
sectors. Polar-decomposed links and the periodic seam keep the result
invariant under local occupied-frame rotations. The result stores signed
Wannier phase paths, minimum Wannier separations, the weakest occupied link,
and the analytic bulk gap. It reports the bounded π-flux quadrupole value 0
or 1/2 only when the bulk and Wannier gaps resolve and both sector
polarizations agree on a quantized value. Otherwise the value is undefined
with an explicit status. Finite corner weight never determines that value.
Earlier saved BBH runs remain readable without Wilson data.

Saved runs reopen with result-hash verification. Standard `.qscene` exports
carry the finite-cell density; the bands view carries exact periodic samples.
Both cite the same verified result SHA-256 and pinned Atlas source. Math3D
reads them independently; it does not run a BBH solver.

## Bounded Kitaev BdG bulk and finite open chain

The `kitaev_chain` native job uses the pinned Atlas spinless nearest-neighbor
model with real pairing. It computes the periodic two-band BdG Hamiltonian in
the `(cₖ, c†₋ₖ)` Nambu basis and diagonalizes a separate finite open chain.
The bounded inputs are chemical potential μ in −4…4, hopping t in 0.25…2,
pairing Δ in 0.1…2, 8–40 open sites and 21–201 momentum samples. Energies
use the same normalized scale; no superconducting material parameters are
inferred.

The result stores both sampled bands, the exact minimum bulk BdG gap on the
continuous one-dimensional Brillouin zone, the sampled gap, and the two
finite eigenenergies closest to zero. Site density averages the particle and
hole probabilities of that finite pair. End weight sums the two exposed
sites. The average avoids assigning a physical distinction to arbitrary
rotations of a degenerate near-zero pair. End localization is a finite-size
observation. The worker does **not** compute a winding or Z₂ invariant,
Majorana operator, or protected phase label.

Saved runs reopen with result-hash verification. Standard `.qscene` exports
contain exact finite-site density; the bands view contains exact periodic
momentum and energy samples. Both cite the same verified result SHA-256 and
pinned Atlas source. Math3D displays them read-only and runs no BdG solver.
