| Coherence and fringes | §§2–2.2, p. 3; §3, p. 4; §4, p. 5; supplement II-A, p. 11 | The original [Andrews et al. interference experiment](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.275.5300.637) recorded fringes from two expanding condensates. The image has density contrast and a spacing. Inferring phase or first-order coherence needs the overlap geometry and imaging model. Visibility is not a direct condensate-fraction measurement; intensity imbalance and shot averaging affect it. The Andrews experiment used sodium, so its numbers are not Rb87 parameters. |
| Release and time of flight | §6.1–6.3, p. 6; §6.4 and §7, p. 7; supplement II-B, p. 11 | Released-cloud images provide spatial widths at observed times. For free motion, the exact variance includes initial position variance, position–momentum covariance, and momentum variance. Only under additional assumptions does width growth isolate initial momentum spread. Mean-field interactions and delta-kick collimation require their own dynamics. An expansion-derived equivalent temperature is an energy scale, not automatically an equilibrium thermometer reading. |
| Imaging | §8.1, p. 7; §8.2, p. 8; supplement II-D, p. 11 | Recorded probe and atom images yield a calibrated intensity ratio. Optical depth and line-of-sight column density are derived from that ratio and an effective cross section. A single absorption image does not directly record a three-dimensional wavefunction, local phase, or full momentum distribution. A far-field time-of-flight image can approximate a momentum map only with ballistic and interaction assumptions. |
| Dated case study | §§9–10, p. 8; §10.1, p. 9; supplement II-E, p. 12 | [Xiong et al., *Science*, published 2026-11-05](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aea5324) reports an optical-dipole-trap condensate aboard the China Space Station, about `2.0 × 10⁴` atoms, `7.0 s` free expansion, and an equivalent temperature. The research team's [Peking University announcement](https://news.pku.edu.cn/info/2881/3325821.htm) identifies the reported figure as `9.7 pK`; the chapter gives `9.7⁺³·²₋₂·₇ pK`. This is an inferred expansion-energy equivalent, not the station's ambient temperature or a verified equilibrium temperature. The published abstract does not claim an operated seven-second interferometer, a gravity sensitivity, or a particular imaging fit. The chapter's width, velocity, cross-section, and temperature worked examples are illustrative, not published measurements from this experiment. |
